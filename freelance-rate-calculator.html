How to calculate your freelance rate

A sustainable freelance rate needs to cover your desired income, business costs, estimated tax reserve, and fees while accounting for unpaid time spent finding clients, doing administration, and managing projects.

  • Annual billable hours: (52 − weeks off) × working hours per week × billable share.
  • Income before tax reserve: target take-home income ÷ (1 − tax reserve rate).
  • Adjusted expenses: annual expenses × (1 + overhead markup).
  • Required billings before your buffer: (income before tax reserve + adjusted expenses) ÷ ((1 − platform fee) × (1 − payout/conversion fee)).
  • Minimum hourly rate: required billings ÷ annual billable hours.
  • Recommended hourly rate: minimum hourly rate × (1 + buffer).
  • Day rate: recommended hourly rate × billable hours per day.

Tips for setting a realistic rate

  • Count only the hours you can reasonably bill, not every hour you work.
  • Include recurring software, equipment, insurance, accounting, and workspace costs.
  • Check the actual platform and payment-provider fees you pay.
  • Choose a tax reserve based on reliable guidance for your location; tax rules vary.
  • Use market benchmarks for comparable skills, experience, project scope, and client type.
  • Review your rates when your expenses, demand, skills, or available working hours change.

Important: This calculator is for general planning and education. It is not tax, accounting, legal, or financial advice. The tax reserve is a simplified percentage estimate and does not model tax brackets, deductions, or local rules.

Frequently asked questions

How much should I charge as a freelancer per hour?

Start with your desired take-home income, business expenses, estimated taxes and fees, then divide the required billings by your realistic annual billable hours. Your experience, specialist skills, client type, and market also affect the rate you can charge.

What is the difference between a minimum rate and a recommended rate?

The minimum rate estimates the hourly amount needed to cover the inputs you entered. The recommended rate adds the optional buffer you choose for profit margin or slower periods.

How many hours should I count as billable?

Count hours you can invoice to clients. Prospecting, proposals, bookkeeping, training, administration, and business development often take time but may not be billable. Use a conservative estimate if you are unsure.

How do platform and payout fees affect my rate?

Fees reduce the share of client billings you keep. This calculator increases the required billings to account for the platform fee and payout or conversion percentage you enter.

Does this calculator calculate my actual taxes?

No. It applies a single percentage as a simplified tax reserve estimate. Actual tax depends on your jurisdiction, income, business structure, deductions, and other circumstances. Consult official guidance or a qualified tax professional.

Can I use this calculator for a day rate or project quote?

Yes. The day rate multiplies your recommended hourly rate by the billable hours per day you enter. The project quote multiplies it by your estimated project hours; adjust for scope risk, revisions, and deliverables as needed.
