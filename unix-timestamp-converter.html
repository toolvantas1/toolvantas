What is Unix epoch time?

Unix time (also known as POSIX time or Epoch time) measures the number of seconds that have elapsed since midnight Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) on January 1, 1970, not counting leap seconds.

  • Universal: It is identical worldwide regardless of your physical location, timezone, or daylight saving time rules.
  • Storage efficient: Storing a single 32-bit or 64-bit integer takes far less database memory than formatting human date strings.
  • Easy arithmetic: Calculating the difference between two timestamps simply requires subtracting one integer from another.

The Year 2038 Problem

Legacy systems storing Unix timestamps as signed 32-bit integers will overflow on January 19, 2038, rolling over to negative numbers and misinterpreting the date as December 13, 1901.

Modern software and 64-bit architectures avoid this limitation entirely, allowing accurate date calculations for approximately 292 billion years into the future.

Frequently asked questions

Does this converter handle milliseconds?

Yes. If you enter an integer greater than 99,999,999,999, it automatically detects that your timestamp is in milliseconds and scales the calculations accordingly.

Why does my timestamp show a different hour than my colleague's?

Unix timestamps are always measured relative to UTC. When rendered as "Local Time", your browser converts the UTC value to your operating system's configured timezone and daylight saving offset.

What is the Unix epoch start date?

The Unix epoch began at 00:00:00 UTC on January 1, 1970, which serves as the universal reference point for computer time tracking.

What is the Year 2038 problem?

It refers to the overflow of 32-bit signed integers storing Unix seconds at 03:14:07 UTC on January 19, 2038. Upgrading systems to 64-bit storage solves this issue permanently.
