How to price a freelance project

A reliable fixed-price quote starts with the deliverables required rather than guesswork. Break the project into discrete tasks, estimate realistic hours, and apply a sustainable hourly rate.

  1. List all deliverables and divide the project into tasks.
  2. Estimate hours for each task and assign an appropriate rate.
  3. Add direct expenses needed to deliver the work.
  4. Include a risk allowance for uncertainty or revision friction.
  5. Gross up for payment fees and agree on a deposit schedule.

Understand the project pricing formula

This calculator separates each component so you can explain your quote transparently:

Labor = sum of (task hours × task rate) Risk allowance = labor × risk percentage Pre-fee amount = labor + expenses + risk allowance Client quote = pre-fee amount ÷ (1 − combined fee rate)

Uncheck the invoice gross-up option if you plan to absorb payment fees yourself instead of building them into the client price.

How much deposit should a freelancer request?

Deposit percentages depend on project duration, upfront costs, client history, and contract norms. Many independent professionals request 25% to 50% upfront before scheduling production work.

Use the deposit field to model upfront cash flow and remaining milestone balance.

Protect scope and revision boundaries

State clearly in your proposal what the fixed price includes: deliverables, included revision rounds, timelines, and what counts as out-of-scope work.

This tool provides planning estimates, not financial, tax, or legal advice. Income tax, VAT/GST, and sales tax are not automatically added.

Frequently asked questions

How much should I charge for a freelance project?

Estimate the hours required for each task, multiply by a suitable rate, add direct expenses and a reasonable allowance for uncertainty, and account for applicable payment fees. Your final quote should also reflect the scope, value, experience, and market for the work.

How do I calculate a freelance project rate?

Break the project into tasks, estimate the hours for each task, and multiply each estimate by its hourly rate. Add the task costs together, then include expenses, any risk allowance, and relevant payment fees to arrive at a fixed-price quote.

Should I charge hourly or use a fixed project price?

Hourly pricing can suit work where scope is uncertain or ongoing. Fixed pricing can suit projects with clear deliverables and boundaries. Choose the model that matches the scope, risk, client expectations, and your contract terms.

How much deposit should I ask a client to pay?

There is no universal percentage. Consider upfront expenses, project length, client risk, payment norms, and any legal or contractual requirements. Agree on the deposit and milestones in writing before work begins.

How do I account for revisions and unexpected work?

Define the number of included revision rounds and the process for approving extra work. You may add a risk allowance to the estimate, but significant scope changes should normally be discussed and priced separately.

Does this calculator convert between currencies or calculate taxes?

No. The currency selector changes the display currency only; it does not convert entered amounts. Income tax and sales tax are not automatically calculated, because tax treatment depends on your circumstances and location.
